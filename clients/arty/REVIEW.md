# Gap review — Arty Pionex plan, capital-first draft

Source: the five-page draft “ARTY Pionex Trade Plan, Capital-First Edition.”
Each row is a rule that was missing, vague, contradictory, or unsafe, and the
fix now in `Arty_Trade_Plan_v2.pdf`.

| Issue | Why it matters | Fix applied |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A “10% weekly account growth” goal, with compounded markers of 550.00, 605.00, 665.50, 732.05 and 5,232.05 after the deposit. | Those dollars read as expected balances. The draft itself said forcing a weekly result leads to oversized bets. The deposit maths also assumed every marker had been hit. | Removed. Progress is checks completed, journal rows, and reviews held. The deposit example uses an unchanged 500.00, so equity is 5,000.00, and says to recompute if the screen differs. |
| “−8% stop” and “+4.8% take-profit” were not tied to a base. A separate line said to leave 5–10% of room beyond the range “for stop/TP logic.” | A beginner cannot tell a percent of bot money from a percent move in the coin. Typing 8 into a price field would be a different, much larger close. | Page 2 table separates the percents. The −8% and +4.8% closes are percents of the money in that bot. The vague 5–10% room is gone. Wick room is 0.5 × daily ATR, inside the range steps. If the Pionex field is a price move, do not type 8 or 4.8. |
| “Liquidation must sit beyond the stop with a meaningful buffer.” No formula, no 3× or 5× distance, “meaningful” untestable. | At 3× a fully built position is about 32.83% from liquidation under a 0.50% maintenance assumption, but the −8% close is only a 2.67% price move. At 5× those figures are 19.50% and 1.60%. A wide range can put liquidation inside the grid. | Formulas, worked prices at a fictional 3,000, and a buffer test: long liquidation ≤ lower × 0.97, short liquidation ≥ upper × 1.03. No preview price means no launch. 5× has six extra gates and is off in weeks 1–2. |
| Sizing said “about 1% of the portfolio” and also “10% of equity,” and mentioned a 12.5% source size that this account should not use. No worked 500 example, no fee or funding in the size check, no platform minimum. | 10% × 8% is 0.80%, not “about 1%” unless someone does the sum. A minimum above 50.00 can break a 1% cap without looking wrong on the screen. | Margin = baseline × cap. Planned stop = margin × 0.08, and that must be ≤ 1% of baseline. Worked: 50.00 margin, 4.00 stop, 2.40 on the +4.8% close. A supposed 70.00 minimum gives 5.60, which is over the 5.00 cap, so the bot is skipped. Fees do not raise size. |
| Loss ladder mixed a weekly −3%, a monthly −6% “next month,” and −10%/−15% “from baseline,” with only the weekly line in dollars (15). Baseline was undefined, so a deposit could hide a loss or reset the halt. | After 4,500 arrives, a percent of the new equity is a different dollar amount. Using the fantasy 732.05 balance would set the wrong caps. | One ladder. At 500.00: week 15.00, window 30.00, soft halt 50.00, hard halt 75.00. Example after a deposit with a zero trading result, equity 5,000.00: 150.00, 300.00, 500.00, 750.00. Trading result = equity − net cash in, so a deposit does not repair a loss. A halt blocks a new baseline. Half means 5%, and the halves do not stack. |
| “Range from Daily, 4H and 1H” had no steps. ETH had an 11% minimum “do not copy,” but other coins had no replacement. “0.3% per grid” can be thinner than a round trip of fees. | Without a width rule, a quiet market gets an invented 11% band, or a trend gets squeezed into a grid. A 0.30% gross step minus a 0.12% round trip leaves 0.18% net, which the fee chart marks as too thin. | Ten numbered steps. ETH minimum is the larger of 11% and 4 × daily ATR%. Other pairs: the larger of 8% and 4 × daily ATR%. Maximum is 8 × daily ATR%. If the minimum is above the maximum, skip. Worst gross step ≥ 0.50% and net of the live round trip ≥ 0.30%. Worked top band on the example is 0.876% gross, 0.756% net, 0.0946 USDT left on a 12.50 order after example costs. Labelled as a cost drawing, not a goal. |
| Launch checks used “approximately,” “unusually large,” “volume supports,” and “acceptable” spread. | None of those can be marked yes or no. | Middle third as prices. Six 4H candles each ≤ 1.5 × 4H ATR(14). Last 24 hourly closes inside the middle half. Spread ≤ 0.08%. Best bid and best ask each ≥ 20 × one grid order. No daily volume in the last five above 2 × the 20-day average. 22 yes/no checks. |
| Funding close was “funding paid exceeds 20% of accumulated grid profit.” | At the start, grid result is zero, so any funding fails the test. Later, a tiny result does the same. | Close if funding paid is greater than the larger of 1.00 USDT and 20% of the realised grid result (use zero if that result is negative). Also do not launch, and close before the next funding time, if the rate is at or beyond 0.050% per 8 hours either way. The fee assumption and the funding test are spelled as different jobs. |
| Exits named a 4H close outside, an 8% candle, “strong volume,” news, and the stop. No time stop, no wick rule, no exchange-outage rule, no definition of strong volume, thin manual-close steps. | A bot can sit outside a useful range for weeks, or a stuck page can be met by adding margin. | Decision picture plus numbers: 2 hours before news, 8% candle, funding rule, 1H ATR over 2.50% or one 1H range over 3%, page stuck 15 minutes, 7 days with fewer than 3 round trips, 14 days absolute. Two outside wicks in 24 hours close the bot. A 4H close in the outer 10% on 2 × average volume closes it. After any close: position flat, orders gone, journal finished, 24-hour wait, new range. Two stops in a row: 48 hours. A liquidation waits for the weekly review. |
| “No revenge trading” and “no adding to a loser” were slogans. Two-stop pause was stated twice with slightly different scope. | A second bot on a down day is how a 15.00 week limit is lost in one session. | Concrete waits, no reuse of old bounds, no size increase, and one definition of a stop-close (the −8% close, a liquidation, or a manual close with a negative result). The launch test adds week loss + 8% of every open bot + 8% of the new bot, and blocks the launch if that exceeds the week limit. Three stops are 12.00, inside 15.00. A fourth planned stop is 16.00, over 15.00. |
| Journal fields lived inside the weekly review, with no daily line and no units. | A review cannot reconstruct fees and funding from the bot headline. | One bot-row field list (symbol copied from the screen, margin mode, ATR, worst step, live fee, liquidation, exit type, fees, funding, rule number) and a five-point daily check, including a line when nothing changed. |
| Weekly review existed. A daily check did not. “Monthly” was only the week-4 heading. “Complete the review” was undefined. | The ladder is useless if nobody measures it on a schedule. | Daily check at a written clock time. Weekly card with blanks, initials, and a rule that the review is unfinished until the card is initialled. Week 4, before any transfer, counts the cards, lists rule breaks, and chooses transfer / transfer but do not use / do not transfer. Until 20 closed bots, a count of winners is not a reason to change size or leverage. |
| Week-4 deposit said to recalculate, but not from which equity, and not how to phase the new size. “Losing bot” was undefined. The printed after-deposit balance assumed the growth markers. | 4,500 USDT on a 500 account is a ninefold jump in the dollar halt. Putting it into a bot that is down is averaging down. | Seven-step protocol. New money goes to equity only. No bot open, none below zero. Hard halt blocks the new table. No new bot for the rest of that week. Next seven days the cap is 5% (250.00 on the 5,000.00 example, planned stop 20.00). Then 10% is 500.00 margin and a 40.00 planned stop. Arty signs that the 4,500 can be lost without borrowing. |
| “DODG” was flagged once. “Market reference” pointed at nothing. ETH was called the volume leader from an unnamed snapshot. | A wrong ticker is an order in the wrong market. A liquidity ranking goes stale. | DODG means DOGE. Five-step ticker check: search, quote must be USDT, neutral futures grid only, copy the symbol from the screen, skip if missing. ETH/USDT in weeks 1–2 is a process rule, not a liquidity claim. No substitute coins. |
| Neutral futures “where appropriate,” leverage “never exceed 5×,” and isolated versus cross were unspecified. Grid count 10–12 did not say intervals or lines. | Futures can liquidate. Cross margin can reach the rest of the account. 5× makes the −8% close a 1.60% price move. | Neutral futures grid only. If that product is missing, skip. It is not swapped for spot or a long/short grid in this month. Arithmetic only. 10, 11, or 12 intervals. Isolated if offered; if cross only, one bot. 3× is the setting. |
| Disclaimer was one short paragraph. It did not say total loss, that examples are not results, that the reader decides, or that Pionex is unaffiliated and must be re-checked. | A first futures account can lose the whole deposit. The draft’s dollar markers could be read as a forecast. | Full disclaimer on the cover, on page 2, and on the sign-off page. A shorter form with the same points is in the footer of every interior page. |
| Sign-off could be read as shared order access. | Stewart must not place or hold keys. | Arty presses every button. Stewart reviews. No account number, password, or API key in the journal or in a message. Screenshot and wait if a label is unclear. |

The draft’s pairs (ZEC, SOL, ETH, NEAR, DOGE, XRP), the 500 USDT start, the 4,500 USDT week-4 decision, the neutral futures grid, arithmetic spacing, 10–12 intervals, 3× default with a 5× ceiling, the −8% and +4.8% closes, one bot then two, and the weekly review with Stewart are kept. The numbers around them are now checks, not hopes.
