# Instructional design for a lesson video

Use this before writing narration. A lesson is finished when the learner can do the thing in the objective on a real example, and can say why.

## Arc

1. Hook. One real situation, in the first seconds. Name the cost of getting the idea wrong.
2. Objective. One sentence with a doing verb: name, sort, count, write, compare. At most three objectives.
3. Sequence. One concept at a time. Define a term, then show it on the real example, then say the limit of the idea.
4. Worked example. Real public numbers, every step on screen, stamped Example. Then one step the learner finishes.
5. Recap. Three lines. Then the next lesson, or a stop if the course is over.

## Anchors

Every key concept sits on at least one of these, shown while it is spoken:

- A real public price path, with the date on screen.
- A real move inside that path (a dip, a leave, a crash), with the date and the price.
- A real-life analogy (a ladder, a fence, a packing list, two buckets), drawn by us and labelled Analogy, placed next to the real path it explains.

Do not leave the analogy alone. The real path has to be on screen in the same idea.

## Picture and voice

- The thing being said is the thing that moves. A level appears when it is named. The playhead arrives on the day being spoken.
- No still frame holds while the sentence changes. The path draws, the playhead travels, a callout lands, or the camera eases.
- Screen text is a short complete phrase. The voice carries the sentences.
- New terms: few. Say the plain words before the label.

## Compliance

Education only. No income claim, no promise a fill will happen, no personal balance, no exchange name, no logo. Example numbers stay marked Example.

## Paid tools

Do not buy a tool from this guide. Speech in this course uses the local Kokoro voice. Pictures of the real path are drawn from `course/data/public-prices/`. Stock photo sites and hosted video avatars are out unless Stewart has already said yes.
