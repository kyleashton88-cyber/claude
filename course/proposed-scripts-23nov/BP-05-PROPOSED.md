## Spoken script

GridBot Builder. GridBot Blueprint. Lesson five. Choosing grid spacing and range.

A gap is the space between two lines. The step is how wide that gap is. They are the same space, said two ways. A range is the top line and the bottom line. This lesson is about choosing those lines and that width from a path you can point at. It does not send an order. Every figure is marked Example. This is education only.

By the end you will count the gaps, not the lines that bound them. You will compare a gap of about 900 with an example cost of about 55. You will widen a range that the real top line already left.

Education only. Not financial advice. This is general information and doesn't consider your objectives, financial situation or needs. Crypto is high risk; you could lose all the money you put in. Past performance is not a reliable indicator of future results. The prices are public historical daily closes, marked Example. No order is sent from this lesson.

A daily close is the last price of the day. The exchange's day ends at 11am Sydney time in daylight saving, and at 10am otherwise. When this lesson says a close stayed inside, it means that close.

Start with the shared Example. From 19 July 2023 to 16 October 2023 the bottom line is 24,930 and the top line is 30,331. Thirty thousand three hundred and thirty-one minus twenty-four thousand nine hundred and thirty is five thousand four hundred and one. There are seven prices on the ladder. The spaces between them are six. Five thousand four hundred and one divided by six is about nine hundred. So the step is about 900. Counting the seven lines and calling them seven gaps is the miss. Seven lines make six gaps.

Now the example cost, near the middle line 27,630. A fee here is a charge the exchange takes when an order trades. This is not a fee copied from an app. It is a teaching cost, marked Example. Suppose the cost is one tenth of one percent on the buy, and the same one tenth of one percent on the sell. One tenth plus one tenth is two tenths. Two tenths of one percent is 0.2 percent. Two tenths of one percent of 27,630 is about 55 dollars. You can see the same sum as 27,630 times 0.002. Twenty-seven thousand six hundred and thirty times two thousandths is 55.26, which we read as about 55. The gap of about 900 is wider than that 55. A gap thinner than the cost is the one you cross out. Wider than the cost is a test you can do on paper. It is not a promise about money.

A tidy round box can still be the wrong range. Suppose the top on the page is the round number 30,000. The real top line in this Example is 30,331. Thirty thousand three hundred and thirty-one minus thirty thousand is three hundred and thirty-one. The real top sits 331 above the round number. A range drawn at 30,000 would have missed the days that reached 30,331. Move the top line up to the real high, then count the gaps again. Do not change the step and the top line in one silent edit. Check the top and the bottom against the path once, not day by day. Every close from 19 July 2023 to 16 October 2023 stayed between 24,930 and 30,331. If your top is below the real top line, move the top line, then count the gaps again.

There are two ways to space the lines, and a beginner should see both on a small ladder before using the summer numbers. Equal dollars means the same number of dollars between each line. Take an Example from 100 to 200, with four gaps. Two hundred minus one hundred is one hundred. One hundred divided by four is twenty-five. The lines are 100, then 125, then 150, then 175, then 200. Each step is 25 dollars. The first gap, from 100 to 125, is the same number of dollars as the last gap, from 175 to 200.

Equal percent means each line is the same fraction higher than the one below it. On the same 100 to 200 Example, four equal percent steps land near 100, then 118.9, then 141.4, then 168.2, then 200. Check one step. One hundred and eighteen point nine minus one hundred is 18.9, which is about 19 percent of 100. One hundred and sixty-eight point two to 200 is 31.8, and 31.8 is about 19 percent of 168.2. The dollars get wider as the price gets higher. The percent stays about the same. The summer Example in this course uses equal dollars of about 900, not equal percent. You do not have to use both on one page. You do have to know which one you wrote.

Say the summer step as a percent only so you can compare it with the cost, not so you can switch methods in the same breath. About 900 divided by 27,630 is about 0.033, which is about 3 percent. The example cost was about 0.2 percent. Three percent is wider than 0.2 percent. That is the same test as 900 against about 55. Mark both figures Example. This is a recount of a public window, not a fee from an app.

Worked recount, in one pass. Lines: seven. Gaps: six. Step: about 900 dollars. Example cost near 27,630: about 55 dollars. Nine hundred is wider than 55, so this step clears that example cost. A thinner gap is the one you cross out. Then look at the top. If it is a round number below 30,331, move it up to 30,331 and count the six gaps again. Stop. You do not need a second pass through the days.

Walk the six gaps as prices, so the word "about 900" is not a fog. Start at the bottom, 24,930. Add about 900 and you are at 25,830. Add about 900 again and you are at 26,730. Add about 900 again and you are at 27,630, the middle, which is also the Example buy level used when a lesson needs one buy line. Add about 900 and you are at 28,530. The published ladder rounds the next two rungs to 28,531 and 29,431, because the real top is 30,331 and the six gaps share 5,401, which is not a perfect stack of 900. Add the last step and you are at 30,331. Count the spaces you just walked. There are six. The lines you named are seven. If your finger counted seven spaces, you counted a line as a gap.

Here is a thin gap, so you can see the cross-out. Suppose the gap was 40 dollars and the example cost was still about 55. Forty is less than 55. That gap is thinner than the cost, so you cross it out. You do not need a story about what happens next. The test is the comparison. Nine hundred against 55 passes this test. Forty against 55 does not. Both figures are Example. Neither one is a fee copied from an app, and neither one is money you made.

One coin-size check belongs here because the gap is a width in dollars per coin. Take one hundredth of a coin, 0.01. A gap of about 900 times 0.01 is 9. That 9 is the gross difference between two neighbouring lines. It is not a result. The example cost of about 55 was measured on a whole coin near 27,630, as 0.2 percent of the price. On 0.01 coin the same 0.2 percent is about 55 cents, because 55 dollars times 0.01 is 0.55 dollars. Nine dollars is wider than 55 cents. The test still passes, and it is still only a test. Mark every one of those figures Example.

One more small ladder, so equal percent is a multiply and not a magic list. Start at 100. The step that turns 100 into 200 in four equal percent jumps is the same multiply each time, about 1.189. One hundred times 1.189 is 118.9. One hundred and eighteen point nine times 1.189 is about 141.4. One hundred and forty-one point four times 1.189 is about 168.2. One hundred and sixty-eight point two times 1.189 is about 200. Four multiplies. Four gaps. Five lines. Same habit as the summer ladder: count the gaps, not the lines. The summer page does not use 1.189. It uses about 900 dollars. You only needed the small ladder so the two methods are not the same word.

Check yourself. How many gaps are there between seven lines? The answer is six. What is the summer step? The answer is about 900, marked Example. What is the example cost near 27,630? The answer is about 55, from 0.1 percent on the buy and 0.1 percent on the sell. Is 900 wider than 55? The answer is yes, on this Example. What is wrong with a top at 30,000? The answer is that the real top line, 30,331, already sits above it.

Point at three things and stop. Point at the top line and say 30,331. Point at the bottom line and say 24,930. Point at one gap and say about 900. Say the lines are the top and the bottom, and the gap is the space. That is the recount. You do not walk the days a second time.

On paper, or on a demo that uses pretend money, write the bottom 24,930 and the top 30,331. Write six gaps of about 900. Write the example cost, about 55, on its own line, and write Example beside every figure. Do not send an order. A recount is not a live plan.

Recap. The top and the bottom come from the real high and the real low. Count the gaps. Six, not seven. A gap that cannot clear the example cost is the one you cross out. Equal dollars and equal percent are two ways to space a ladder. This Example uses equal dollars.
