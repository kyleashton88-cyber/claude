## Spoken script

GridBot Builder. GridBot Blueprint. Lesson three. What is a grid bot.

Education only. Not financial advice. This is general information and doesn't consider your objectives, financial situation or needs. Crypto is high risk; you could lose all the money you put in. Past performance is not a reliable indicator of future results. The prices are public historical daily prices, marked Example. The range uses the high and the low of that window. When we say inside or outside, we use the daily close. No order is sent from this lesson.

A grid bot is a program that places orders that wait inside a top and a bottom you already measured. That top and that bottom are the range. A limit order is an order that waits at a price you choose. A market order does not wait. It buys or sells straight away, at the prices people are already offering. This lesson shows how those waiting orders sit on a page. It does not send an order.

By the end you will know what a grid bot is. You will know which side of the middle buys and which side sells. You will know why the price touching a line is not the same as a result. Every figure in this lesson is marked Example. This is education only.

An order is an instruction to buy or sell that can wait for a price. A fill is an order that actually trades. A rung is one price line where an order waits. The levels are those rungs. A gap is the space between two levels. The step is how wide that gap is. The range is the top line and the bottom line. On the shared Example the bottom line is 24,930 and the top line is 30,331.

A daily close is the last price of the day. This lesson uses the end of each UTC day, the world's standard clock, which is 11am Sydney time in daylight saving, 10am otherwise. We use that close, not a price from the middle of the night, when we say the price touched a line.

Here is the gap arithmetic, marked Example. Thirty thousand three hundred and thirty-one minus twenty-four thousand nine hundred and thirty is five thousand four hundred and one. Five thousand four hundred and one divided by six gaps is about nine hundred. So this window has seven levels and six gaps of about 900. Count the gaps, not the lines. Seven lines are not seven gaps.

The middle level in this Example is 27,630. Buys wait below the middle. Sells wait above the middle. One gap of about 900 below 27,630 is 26,730. Two gaps below is 25,830. Three gaps below is 24,930, the bottom line. One gap above 27,630 is 28,531. That gap is 901, not 900, because the six gaps share 5,401. Two gaps above is 29,431. The top line, 30,331, sits above that. Those are levels. They are not orders until you write them, and this lesson does not write a live order.

A cross is the price line touching a rung. A fill is the order actually trading. A ring on a teaching chart can mean the price touched the rung. A white square can mean an order traded. Do not mix those marks. On this Example chart the daily close crossed the middle rung, 27,630, ten times. Those ten marks are crosses, not fills. They are marked Example. They are not money you made, and they are not a count of finished pairs.

On 18 August 2023 the Example close was 26,050. Twenty-seven thousand six hundred and thirty minus twenty-six thousand and fifty is one thousand five hundred and eighty. The close was below the middle, so that day sits on the buy side of the page. Below the middle is where buy levels wait. Above the middle is where sell levels wait. The date does not by itself make a fill.

From 19 July 2023 to 16 October 2023 the Example path stayed between 24,930 and 30,331. The daily close crossed the middle ten times and still finished inside the range. That path is the picture next to the ladder. It is marked Example. It is not a forecast, and it is not a reason to start.

Now one step, with the arithmetic in the open. Coin is the crypto, such as bitcoin. Cash is the money you can spend. In this Example we use one hundredth of a coin, which is 0.01. A gap of about 900 times 0.01 is 9. That 9 is the gross difference between a buy level and the sell level one gap higher. It is not a result. Fees are not in this 9. The later lesson tests a gap against a fee.

Say the same step as prices. The Example buy level one gap under the middle is 26,730. Twenty-six thousand seven hundred and thirty times 0.01 is 267 dollars and 30 cents. That is the coin size times the buy price. The Example sell level one gap higher is 27,630. Twenty-seven thousand six hundred and thirty times 0.01 is 276 dollars and 30 cents. That is the coin size times the sell price. Two hundred and seventy-six dollars and 30 cents minus 267 dollars and 30 cents is 9 dollars. Nine dollars is the same gross you got from gap times coin size. The locked fee on this same Example pair is 0.1 percent of each side. 26,730 times 0.001 times 0.01 is about 27 cents. 27,630 times 0.001 times 0.01 is about 28 cents. Together that is 0.5436 dollars, about US$0.54. Nine dollars minus about 54 cents is about 8 dollars 46. That net is still not money you made. It is the teaching sum only if both orders fill and the fee is that Example cost. Mark every one of those figures Example. A cross at the buy line is not this trade. A fill is the order actually trading. This picture does not promise that the sell arrives. The tax office treats a swap like a sale, so a fill can belong in the journal. This is general information, not tax advice.

Inventory is how much coin you hold against how much cash. After the Example buy fills, you hold a little more coin and a little less cash. A sell can be written one rung higher. If that sell fills, you hold a little less coin and a little more cash. One buy and the later sell of that same coin is one pair. The pair is complete only when both have filled. Other buys can still be waiting. Waiting is not a finished pair. A fill only swaps cash and coin. In hindsight, on this finished window, that swap is a count you can point at. It is not a forecast. Whether you are ahead depends on the later sell, the fee, and where the price goes next. This course does not say a bot earns while the price moves back and forth. That sentence would be a promise. We do not make it.

The Example settings a program asks for are the top, the bottom, and how many gaps. If a later lesson ever writes a size, a program works it out as the money divided by the number of orders. For example only, 600 dollars across 6 orders is 100 dollars each. Six hundred divided by six is one hundred. That is arithmetic, not a size for your account, and size stays off the page until the finish rule exists. The program only places and refills what you wrote. It does not decide the stop unless you have written that its stop setting is on. An API secret is a key that lets an app place trades on your account, and sometimes move money out of it. Keep withdrawal permissions off. This lesson still sends nothing. A stop is the rule for what you will do if a daily close finishes outside the range and the next daily close is still outside.

This Example ended in October 2023. On 23 October 2023 the daily close was 33,086. Thirty-three thousand and eighty-six minus thirty thousand three hundred and thirty-one is two thousand seven hundred and fifty-five. The close was above the top line. The next day's close was still above it, so the stop was confirmed then, not by looking back later. On the way up, the sells can fill and the page can sit in cash while the price rises. A fall through the bottom would leave you holding coin. You write the stop before you start. You do not invent it after the close has left.

Take one more Example, still on paper. Price steps down through two buy levels. The first buy is at 26,730. Times 0.01, that is 267 dollars and 30 cents of cash swapped for coin. The second buy is at 25,830. Times 0.01, that is 258 dollars and 30 cents. Add them. Two hundred and sixty-seven dollars and 30 cents plus 258 dollars and 30 cents is 525 dollars and 60 cents. That is the cash used for 0.02 coin. You now hold more coin and less cash. A sell can be written one gap above each buy. The lower buy, 25,830, has a sell waiting at 26,730. The higher buy, 26,730, has a sell waiting at 27,630.

If the price rises only as far as 26,730, the lower sell can fill and the higher sell is still waiting. Gap times coin size is again 900 times 0.01, which is 9 dollars gross on that one pair. The other 0.01 coin is still held. One pair is complete. One parcel is waiting. Waiting is not a second pair. Mark the 9 dollars Example. Do not call it money you made. The fee is not in the 9, and the price may never reach the higher sell.

Say the cycle in order, slowly. Price steps down and a buy can fill. You hold more coin and less cash. A sell can be written one rung higher. Price steps up and that sell can fill. You hold less coin and more cash. A buy can be written one rung lower. That is one completed pair only if both orders traded. On the Example daily closes from July to October 2023, a one-step cycle of that kind finished only three times. Those three pairs are 26,730 with 27,630, then 25,830 with 26,730, then 26,730 with 27,630 again. This lesson's book puts buys only below the middle, 27,630. Under that book, those three pairs complete, and zero buys are still waiting for a sell when the window ends. Hindsight: three is a count of that public window. It is not a promise that the next window will do the same. A different opening book, with buys also above the middle, is not the book this lesson teaches.

Check yourself. What is a limit order? The answer is: an order that waits at a price you choose. Which side of 27,630 is the buy side? The answer is: below the middle. How many gaps are there between seven levels? The answer is: six. What is ten crosses of the middle? The answer is: ten touches, not ten fills, and not a result. All of those figures are Example.

On paper, or on a demo that uses pretend money, draw the top 30,331 and the bottom 24,930. Mark the middle 27,630. Put three buy levels below it and three sell levels above it. Write one stop sentence. Do not send an order from this lesson. A practice sketch is not a live plan. Write Example on the page so nobody can read it as an order.

Recap. A grid here means limit orders waiting inside the range you measured. Buys wait below the middle. Sells wait above the middle. A cross is a touch. A fill is a trade. When the Example close left the range in October, that picture no longer applied.
