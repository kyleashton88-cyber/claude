## Spoken script

GridBot Builder. GridBot Blueprint. Lesson four. Market regimes.

Education only. Not financial advice. This is general information and doesn't consider your objectives, financial situation or needs. Crypto is high risk; you could lose all the money you put in. Past performance is not a reliable indicator of future results. The prices are public historical daily prices, marked Example. The range uses the high and the low of that window. When we say inside or outside, we use the daily close. No order is sent from this lesson.

A regime is the shape of the price path. It is not a mood, and it is not a forecast. You name the shape only after you can point at the dates. This lesson teaches three shapes. Range, in this course, means only the top line and the bottom line. A ranging market is a path that keeps coming back inside that range. The word range does not pick up a second meaning here. A trend is a path that leaves and keeps going. A break is a jump out of the lines you drew. The same path can be more than one of these if you change the dates. Every figure is marked Example. This is education only.

By the end you will be able to tell a sideways market, a trend, and a break apart. You will also know that one market can be more than one of them, depending on the window you chose. You will not be asked to predict the next day.

A daily close is the last price of the day. This lesson uses the end of each UTC day, the world's standard clock, which is 11am Sydney time in daylight saving, 10am otherwise. When this lesson says the price was inside or outside, it means that close, not a price from the middle of the night.

Start with the shared Example. From 19 July 2023 to 16 October 2023 the bottom line is 24,930 and the top line is 30,331. Thirty thousand three hundred and thirty-one minus twenty-four thousand nine hundred and thirty is five thousand four hundred and one. Five thousand four hundred and one divided by six gaps is about nine hundred. So one gap is about 900. Every daily close in that window finished inside those two lines. That is what we mean by a ranging market on these dates. The path came back and stayed inside the range, meaning the top line and the bottom line. Write the dates next to the words ranging market. A ranging-market label with no dates is not a label you can check.

Inside that range, "near the top" means within one gap of the top line. Thirty thousand three hundred and thirty-one minus about 900 is about 29,431. A close from about 29,431 up to 30,331 is near the top. "Near the bottom" means within one gap of the bottom line. Twenty-four thousand nine hundred and thirty plus about 900 is about 25,830. A close from 24,930 up to about 25,830 is near the bottom. Count the Example closes that way and you get eight near the top and five near the bottom. It is not four and four. The two ends of a range do not have to match. If your page says four and four, recount before you move on.

The middle of this Example is 27,630. A close under the middle is on the buy side of the page. A close over the middle is on the sell side. That tells you where the close sat. It does not tell you the shape. Shape comes from whether the closes stay inside the two lines, leave and keep going, or jump.

Now change the dates, and the shape changes. On 23 October 2023 the Example daily close was 33,086. Thirty-three thousand and eighty-six minus thirty thousand three hundred and thirty-one is two thousand seven hundred and fifty-five. That close was above the top line of the earlier range. By 14 March 2024 the Example close was 71,397. Seventy-one thousand three hundred and ninety-seven minus thirty-three thousand and eighty-six is thirty-eight thousand three hundred and eleven. The path had left and it kept going. On those later dates the shape is a trend, not the ranging market you labelled in the summer window. One path can leave the range you measured and start a trend. That leave on 23 October 2023 is the break. The later path, through 14 March 2024, is the trend. You do not erase the earlier label. You write both, each with its own dates.

A break is the jump itself. The close steps outside the lines in one move, rather than drifting out over many days. You still confirm it the way the earlier lesson set the stop. A stop is the rule for what you will do if a daily close finishes outside the range and the next daily close is still outside. One close outside is a warning. The next close still outside is the confirmation in this course. You wrote that rule before the jump. You do not invent it after you have seen the jump.

Look at a break that is not the summer window. On 11 March 2020 the Example close was about 7,911. On 12 March 2020 it was about 4,971, and the 13 March close was higher. So 12 March to 13 March is not the drop. The card holds the rest of that candle: about 37 percent from the 11 March close to the 12 March close, and a 13 March width of 1,731. If a page says the market fell from the 12th to the 13th, the close says otherwise. Read the close before you name the shape.

A candle is one day's picture. The high is the highest price that day. The low is the lowest. The close is the last price. This lesson uses the end of each UTC day, the world's standard clock, which is 11am Sydney time in daylight saving, 10am otherwise. A long candle can look like a break even when the close came back inside. That is why this course reads the close, then the next close, before it treats a jump as confirmed.

Here is how you label a window so you can check it later. Write the shape, then the first date, then the last date. "Ranging market, 19 July 2023 to 16 October 2023." "Trend, 23 October 2023 to 14 March 2024." If you cannot point at the closes that match the word, write "not sorted" and the dates you looked at. "Not sorted" is an honest label. A guessed label is not. Do not write a shape because a picture feels calm or fast. Count the closes against the lines.

A ranging market is the shape a spot grid is built for, and only on the dates where the closes actually stayed inside the range. Spot means you buy the coin with money you already have. A contract is a trade on the price, often with borrowed money. This lesson does not use one. That other kind of trading can be a financial product, and it is higher risk. This course does not teach it and does not recommend it. This lesson does not switch the example into that other kind of trading. If the shape on your dates is a trend or a break, the range you drew no longer matches the path. The stop you wrote is the response. A new range is a new measurement, with new dates, done before any new order.

Work one window in full before you name it. Step one: write the top and the bottom. Step two: write what one gap is. In the Example, about 900. Step three: mark every daily close as inside, near the top, near the bottom, or outside. Step four: count them. Step five: write the shape and the dates, or write "not sorted". On the summer Example that count is: every close inside, eight near the top, five near the bottom. On the later Example the first close is already outside, and the path keeps going, so the shape is a trend on those dates.

Take one close that is inside and still not near either end. On 7 October 2023 the Example daily close was 27,969. The middle is 27,630. Twenty-seven thousand nine hundred and sixty-nine minus twenty-seven thousand six hundred and thirty is three hundred and thirty-nine. That is less than one gap of about 900, so the close is a little above the middle. It is not near the top. Near the top starts at about 29,431, and 27,969 is below that. It is not near the bottom either. Near the bottom ends at about 25,830, and 27,969 is above that. So this close is inside, on the sell side of the page, and in the middle of the count. It does not add to the eight near the top or the five near the bottom. A close can be inside without being near an end. Mark 27,969 Example. It is not an order.

Check yourself. What is a regime? The answer is: the shape of the path, named only with dates. What is a ranging market? The answer is: a path that keeps coming back inside a top and a bottom. What is a range, in this course? The answer is: only the top line and the bottom line. In the July to October 2023 Example, how many closes were near the top, and how many near the bottom? The answer is: eight near the top and five near the bottom. Not four and four, and not four and three. Was 12 to 13 March 2020 the drop? The answer is: no. The close on 13 March was higher than the close on 12 March. The drop in that Example was from about 7,911 on 11 March to about 4,971 on 12 March. What do you write when you cannot show the closes? The answer is: "not sorted", plus the dates.

On paper, or on a demo that uses pretend money, copy two windows. First the summer range, 24,930 to 30,331, with the eight-and-five count written in words. Then the later path, from 33,086 to 71,397, labelled trend with its dates. Do not send an order. A label is not a plan. Write Example on both pages.

Recap. A regime is a shape plus dates. A ranging market stays inside the range, meaning the top and the bottom. A trend leaves and keeps going. A break is the jump, confirmed by the next daily close still outside, which is the stop you already wrote. One path can be a ranging market on one pair of dates and a trend on the next. If you cannot show the closes, write "not sorted".
