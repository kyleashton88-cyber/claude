## Spoken script

GridBot Builder. GridBot Blueprint. Lesson eight. Managing and monitoring.

Education only. Not financial advice. This is general information and doesn't consider your objectives, financial situation or needs. Crypto is high risk; you could lose all the money you put in. Past performance is not a reliable indicator of future results. The prices are public historical daily prices, marked Example. The range uses the high and the low of that window. When we say inside or outside, we use the daily close. No order is sent from this lesson.

Monitoring is three questions you can finish, not something you watch all day. Inside means the close is still between the top and the bottom. Which side means whether the close is above or below the middle. The third question uses two different words, so they do not get mixed. In force means the finish rule is still the rule, and the stop has not fired. Fired means the stop has been met. We will ask the questions on 7 October 2023, then on 23 October 2023, so you can see the answers change. Once a week, while a page is running, take the latest daily close and ask the same three questions. Lesson 1 called that the weekly check. These two dates are the practice. The habit is the week. This is education only. Every figure is marked Example.

By the end you will answer three questions: inside, which side, and whether the rule is still in force or the stop has fired. You will use 7 October and 23 October as the two practice checks. You will know to ask the same three questions once a week.

A daily close is the last price of the day. This lesson uses the end of each UTC day, the world's standard clock, which is 11am Sydney time in daylight saving, 10am otherwise. The range in this Example is 24,930 to 30,331. The middle is 27,630. A stop is the rule you wrote: if a daily close finishes outside the range and the next daily close is still outside, the example is over.

7 October 2023. The public close was 27,969. Inside the range? Yes. That close sits between 24,930 and 30,331. Which side of 27,630? Above it, by a little. Twenty-seven thousand nine hundred and sixty-nine minus twenty-seven thousand six hundred and thirty is three hundred and thirty-nine. Three hundred and thirty-nine is the distance above the middle. It is not a result. Is the finish rule still in force? Yes. The path has not left 30,331 yet, so the stop has not fired. Three answers. That week's check is done. You do not keep the chart open to see what the next hour does. You ask again next week. This check uses the daily close.

23 October 2023. The public close was 33,086. Inside the summer range? No. Thirty-three thousand and eighty-six minus thirty thousand three hundred and thirty-one is two thousand seven hundred and fifty-five. The close is 2,755 above the top line. Once the price has left the range, which side of the middle no longer matters. The close is not between the lines, so "above the middle" is not the question any more. Has the stop fired? Yes. The finish rule has been met. The close left 30,331, and the next day's close was still outside. It is not still in force as an untriggered rule. The action that follows is the sentence you already wrote, not a new hunt for a score.

You do not ask the three questions of every day in the window. Two dates are enough to see the change: one still inside, and one that has left. When the three answers are written, you stop. A longer list of days would not teach a new idea. It would only repeat the same three questions.

The fourth row is a review record, not a target. A review record is a note you write after the check, so you can see what the page did. The words on that row are these. Realised means a pair that has finished, both the buy and the later sell. Unrealised means coin you still hold, priced at the latest close, which can change. Fees means the charges the exchange took. The last note is whether you followed the rules you wrote. None of those words is a score you are trying to beat. None of them is a promise. You can leave the fourth row blank in this lesson and still have done the check. The three answers are the check. The fourth row is only a place to write later.

We do not operate an alert service. An alert would be a message that arrives when a price hits a line. This lesson does not send one. You open the page on the two dates, you write the three answers, and you close it.

Say both dates once more, with the arithmetic in the open, so the card and the voice match. On 7 October the close is 27,969. It is inside. It is 339 above 27,630. The stop has not fired. The rule is still in force. On 23 October the close is 33,086. It is outside. It is 2,755 above 30,331. The stop has fired. That week's check is done. Ask again next week.

Say what the fourth row is not, because the words are easy to turn into a score. Realised, on this row, only means a pair that has finished, both the buy and the later sell. It is not money you made. Unrealised only means coin you still hold, priced at the latest close so you can see it change. It is not a tax figure. The tax office may treat a fill or a swap as a sale. This is general information, not tax advice. You can leave the fourth row blank in this lesson. Fees are not a surprise you discover at the end. They are the charges you already compared with the gap: about US$0.54 on 0.01 coin, which is 54.36 on a whole coin at the two taught prices. Whether you followed the rules is a yes or a no about the page you wrote. It is not a grade of you.

A check you cannot finish is not monitoring. It is just watching. These two dates are the whole practice. Write the three answers. Use the fourth row only if you want the review record. Then close the card and leave the chart. The same three questions on a later day would be the same kind of check. You do not need a row for every day to understand the change from 7 October to 23 October.

Here is the inside test as a pair of comparisons, so "inside" is not a feeling. Is 27,969 greater than 24,930? Yes. Is 27,969 less than 30,331? Yes. Both answers yes means inside. Is 33,086 less than 30,331? No. One no means outside. You can do that with any later close. The two dates are the ones this lesson asks you to learn.

Write the card in the order you will say it, so the check has an ending. Line one: the date. Line two: inside or not, with the two comparisons. Line three: which side of 27,630, and only if the close is inside. Line four: in force, or fired. On 7 October, line three is allowed, and the answer is above, by 339. Line four is in force. The stop has not fired. On 23 October, you skip line three, because the close is outside, and line four is fired. When line four is written, that week's check is done. You open the notebook again the next week and ask the same three questions of the new daily close.

If you want the fourth row, label it before you write in it. Write the words "review record, not a target" at the top of that row. Then you may note a finished pair, coin still held, fees, and a yes or no for the rules. If you cannot say which of those four notes you are writing, leave the row blank. A blank review row is allowed. A blank answer to "inside or not" is not.

Check yourself. What are the three questions? The answer is: inside or not, which side of the middle, and whether the rule is still in force or the stop has fired. On 7 October 2023, is 27,969 inside 24,930 to 30,331? The answer is yes. On 23 October 2023, does which side of the middle still matter? The answer is no. The close has left the range. What is the fourth row? The answer is a review record, not a target.

One more pass, spoken as a card you could hand to someone. Card A, 7 October 2023. Close 27,969. Inside, because it is above 24,930 and below 30,331. Side, above 27,630 by 339. Rule, still in force. The stop has not fired. Card B, 23 October 2023. Close 33,086. Outside, because 33,086 is not below 30,331. Side, not asked. Rule, the stop has fired, and the next close was still outside. Hand the two cards over. If the other person can read the action from card B without asking you what you meant, the sentences are done. If they ask "and then what", the action is the sentence you wrote before you started, not a new one you invent while handing the card over.

On paper, or on a demo that uses pretend money, write the two dates and the three answers. Write Example beside both closes. Do not send an order. A finished check is not a new plan.

The middle is a side marker, not a third line of the range. The range is still 24,930 and 30,331. 27,630 only answers "which side" when the close is inside. On 7 October, 27,969 is 339 above it, so the side is above. On 23 October you do not use 27,630 at all. Using the middle on an outside day makes the page look as if the close were still in the range. It is not. Skip that question and go to the rule.

Recap. Ask: inside or not, which side of the middle, and whether the rule is still in force or the stop has fired. 7 October was inside, and the rule was still in force. 23 October was outside, and the stop had fired. Ask the same three questions once a week. These two dates are the practice, not the only days the habit exists.

Put the two distances where you can see them and then close the book. 339 is how far 27,969 sits above 27,630. 2,755 is how far 33,086 sits above 30,331. The first distance is inside the range. The second distance is outside it. You do not add them. You do not average them. Each one answers its own date. When both answers are written, monitoring for this lesson is finished. Watching a third day would repeat the method without teaching it.
