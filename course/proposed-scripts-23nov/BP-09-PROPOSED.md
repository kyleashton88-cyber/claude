## Spoken script

GridBot Builder. GridBot Blueprint. Lesson nine. Common mistakes.

Education only. Not financial advice. This is general information and doesn't consider your objectives, financial situation or needs. Crypto is high risk; you could lose all the money you put in. Past performance is not a reliable indicator of future results. The prices are public historical daily prices, marked Example. The range uses the high and the low of that window. When we say inside or outside, we use the daily close. No order is sent from this lesson.

The mistakes in this lesson are pages, not personalities. A blank stop. A range tighter than the real top line. A line count used as a gap count. Each one can be pointed at on the summer chart. You fix one, you read the page again, and then you fix the next. Every figure is marked Example. This is education only.

By the end you will find a missing stop, a range tighter than the real top line, and a line counted as a gap. You will fix one, reread, then fix the next. You will keep result language off the page.

A stop is the written rule for what you will do if a daily close finishes outside the range and the next daily close is still outside. A daily close is the last price of the day. This lesson uses the end of each UTC day, the world's standard clock, which is 11am Sydney time in daylight saving, 10am otherwise. The range in this Example is 24,930 to 30,331. A gap is the space between two lines. There are seven lines and six gaps of about 900.

A blank stop means the finish column was skipped. Without a written rule for a close outside the range, every later copy of the summer range has no end. The fix is to write the sentence, not to add another chart tool. The sentence you can point at is the one from lesson six. If a daily close finishes outside the range and the next daily close is still outside, the example is over. The date is 23 October 2023. The Example close was 33,086. Thirty-three thousand and eighty-six minus thirty thousand three hundred and thirty-one is two thousand seven hundred and fifty-five. The close was above the top, and the next close was still outside. Write that. Do not write a result word next to it.

A tight range here is a round box. It looks tidy. Suppose the top on the page is 30,000. The real top line is 30,331. Thirty thousand three hundred and thirty-one minus thirty thousand is three hundred and thirty-one. The real top sits 331 above the round box. The fix is to move the top line up to the real high, then recount six gaps. You do not fix the top line and the count in one scribble. Move the top. Read the page. Then count.

Seven prices are on the ladder. The gaps between them are six. Calling it a seven-grid because you counted seven lines is the same counting mistake from lesson five. The fix is the recount you already did: six. Seven marks. Six gaps. If a form uses the word grids for gaps, you still enter six, because your page has six gaps.

Ask one question of the whole window, not of every day. Does the page match the path? The path stayed between 24,930 and 30,331 until the close left on 23 October 2023. If the page says something else, rewrite the page. A page that says four and four, or four and three, near the two ends is already wrong. The Example count is eight near the top and five near the bottom. A close is near the top when it is at least about 29,431. A close is near the bottom when it is at most about 25,830. On the ninety daily closes from 19 July to 16 October 2023, that rule gives eight and five. Two other prices inside the window sit just above 25,830, so they are not in the bottom count. 10 September closed at 25,832. 12 September closed at 25,833. Those are prices, not the next days after the window. That is a recount from the course data, not a grade.

Worked audit, two passes. Pass one: write the missing stop rule for 23 October 2023, confirmed when the next close was still outside. Pass two: move a round top of 30,000 up to the real top line, 30,331, because the path had already gone past the round number. Then recount. Do not grade the page. List the rewrites. A result word does not belong on either pass. The page is an Example, not a score.

After each fix, read the three words again. Range: the real bottom line to the real top line, past a round box. Levels: seven rungs, which make six gaps of about 900, not seven gaps. Stop: a close outside the range, confirmed by the next close, which this path met when the next close after 23 October 2023 was still outside. If one of those three is still blank, that is the next fix. Fix those three, in that order, and the page matches the summer path.

Read a wrong page and a corrected page, line by line, so the fixes are not a slogan. The wrong page says: top 30,000, seven gaps, and no stop sentence. The corrected page says: top 30,331, bottom 24,930, six gaps of about 900, and a stop sentence that points at 23 October 2023 and the next close still outside. The bottom did not need a fix in this Example, because 24,930 is already the real low of that window. The top did. The count did. The stop did. Three fixes. Not one scribble.

After the recount, say the ladder once so six is a count you can see. From 24,930 the lines step by about 900: 25,830, then 26,730, then 27,630, then 28,531, then 29,431, then 30,331. That is seven lines. The spaces are six. If the corrected page still says seven gaps, the third fix is not done. Go back and change the word gaps, not the word lines.

A result word is any word that treats the page as a score. Do not write one on the audit. Do not write one on the stop. Do not write one on the recount. The page shows a public window and the rules you would have written. It does not show what you made. If a sentence you just wrote sounds like a score, delete the sentence and keep the date.

One mistake often hides inside a fix, so split the edits. Pass one is only the stop. Pass two changes only the top. Pass three changes only the gap count. If you fix them together, you cannot tell which edit matched the path.

The summer path is the judge, not the neatness of the box. Every close from 19 July 2023 to 16 October 2023 stayed between 24,930 and 30,331. A page that is rounder than that path is the page that is wrong. A page that is complete and a little untidy, with the real top and a written stop, is the page that matches.

Check yourself. What is the first miss? The answer is a missing finish rule. What is wrong with a top at 30,000? The answer is that 30,331 is 331 above it. How many gaps are seven lines? The answer is six. What word stays off the page? The answer is a result word. The page is an Example, not a grade.

Here is the corrected page in the words you will read back. Range: 24,930 to 30,331. Not 30,000. Levels: seven rungs. Gaps: six, about 900, which is 5,401 divided by six. Seven rungs, six gaps. Stop: a daily close outside that range, confirmed by the next daily close still outside. This path met that rule on 23 October 2023, close 33,086, which is 2,755 above the top, and the next close was still outside. If those three sentences are on the page, the misses in this lesson are fixed. If the top is right and the stop is blank, you are not finished. Go back to pass one. The order is the method.

On paper, or on a demo that uses pretend money, write the three fixes in order. Stop sentence. Then the real top, 30,331. Then six gaps. Write Example on the page. Do not send an order. A corrected page is not a live plan.

The gap arithmetic is the check on the third fix. Five thousand four hundred and one, which is 30,331 minus 24,930, divided by six, is about 900. If the corrected page says six gaps and also says the step is 300, the page disagrees with itself. Six times 300 is 1,800, and 1,800 is not 5,401. The step has to fit the width. About 900 times six is about 5,400, which matches the width of this Example. That is how you know the recount is the same ladder, not a new one drawn in a hurry.

Recap. A missing finish rule is the first miss. A range tighter than the real top line is the second. A line count standing in for a gap count is the third. Fix one, read the page, then fix the next. A result word stays off every pass. The corrected numbers are 24,930, 30,331, six gaps of about 900, and the stop dated 23 October 2023. If a number on the page is not one of those, ask whether the path put it there. If it did not, take it off.

Say the three misses in the order you fix them, one breath each. First, a blank stop, fixed by the 23 October 2023 sentence. Second, a top at 30,000, fixed by moving it to 30,331, which is 331 higher. Third, seven counted as gaps, fixed by six. Then read range, levels, and stop. If you can do that without a result word, the audit is done. You do not add a fourth miss to make the page longer.
