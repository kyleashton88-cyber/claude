## Spoken script

GridBot Builder. GridBot Blueprint. Lesson eight. Managing and monitoring.

Education only. Not financial advice. This is general information and doesn't consider your objectives, financial situation or needs. Crypto is high risk; you could lose all the money you put in. Past performance is not a reliable indicator of future results. The prices are public historical daily prices, marked Example. The range uses the high and the low of that window. When we say inside or outside, we use the daily closing price. No order is sent from this lesson.

Monitoring is three questions you can finish, not something you watch all day. Inside means the closing price is still from the bottom line to the top line, including a closing price that sits on either line. Which side means whether the closing price is above or below the middle. The third question uses two different words, so they do not get mixed. In force means the finish rule is still the rule, and the stop has not fired. Fired means the stop has been met. We will ask the questions on 7 October 2023, then on 23 October 2023, so you can see the answers change. Once a week, while a page is running, read every daily closing price since your last check, about seven, and look for two in a row outside, then ask the same three questions of the latest one. Lesson 1 called that the weekly check. These two dates are the practice. The habit is the week. This is education only. Every figure is marked Example.

By the end you will answer three questions: inside, which side, and whether the rule is still in force or the stop has fired. You will use 7 October and 23 October as the two practice checks. You will know to ask the same three questions once a week while the rule is still in force.

A daily closing price is the last price of the day. This lesson uses the end of each UTC day, the world's standard clock, which is 11am Sydney time in daylight saving, 10am otherwise. The range in this Example is 24,930 to 30,331. The middle is 27,630. A stop is the rule you wrote: if a daily closing price finishes outside the range and the next daily closing price is still outside, the example is over.

7 October 2023. The public closing price was 27,969. Inside the range? Yes. That closing price sits between 24,930 and 30,331. Which side of 27,630? Above it, by a little. Twenty-seven thousand nine hundred and sixty-nine minus twenty-seven thousand six hundred and thirty is three hundred and thirty-nine. Three hundred and thirty-nine is the distance above the middle. It is not a result. Is the finish rule still in force? Yes. The path has not left 30,331 yet, so the stop has not fired. Three answers. That week's check is done. You do not keep the chart open to see what the next hour does. You ask again next week. This check reads every daily closing price since the last check.

23 October 2023. The public closing price was 33,086. Inside the summer range? No. Thirty-three thousand and eighty-six minus thirty thousand three hundred and thirty-one is two thousand seven hundred and fifty-five. The closing price is 2,755 above the top line. Once the price has left the range, which side of the middle no longer matters. The closing price is not between the lines, so "above the middle" is not the question any more. Has the stop fired? Not yet. The closing price left 30,331, and this first closing price outside is the warning. The stop fires only if the next closing price is still outside. The next closing price, on 24 October 2023, was 33,902, still outside, so the stop fired on 24 October. The action that follows is the sentence you already wrote, not a new hunt for a score.

You do not ask the three questions of every day in the window. Two dates are enough to see the change: one still inside, and one that has left. When the three answers are written, you stop. A longer list of days would not teach a new idea. It would only repeat the same three questions.

The fourth row is a review record, not a target. You can leave it blank in this lesson and still have done the check. The three answers are the check.

We do not operate an alert service. An alert would be a message that arrives when a price hits a line. This lesson does not send one. You open the page once a week, while the rule is still in force, and read every daily closing price since the last check. In this lesson you practise on two dates. You write the three answers, and you close it.

Say both dates once more, with the arithmetic in the open, so the card and the voice match. On 7 October the closing price is 27,969. It is inside. It is 339 above 27,630. The stop has not fired. The rule is still in force. On 23 October the closing price is 33,086. It is outside. It is 2,755 above 30,331. That is the warning. On 24 October the closing price is 33,902, still outside, so the stop has fired. That week's check is done. You do not ask the same three questions of this page next week. You follow the sentence you already wrote.

You may leave the fourth row blank. Realised and unrealised can wait until a later example. The tax office may treat a fill or a swap as a sale. This is general information, not tax advice. Whether you followed the rules is a yes or a no about the page you wrote. It is not a grade of you. The fee test stays on its own card, as about 54 cents on 0.01 coin.

A check you cannot finish is not monitoring. It is just watching. These two dates are the whole practice. Write the three answers. Use the fourth row only if you want the review record. Then close the card and leave the chart. The same three questions on a later day would be the same kind of check. You do not need a row for every day to understand the change from 7 October to 23 October.

Here is the inside test as a pair of comparisons, so "inside" is not a feeling. Is 27,969 at least 24,930? Yes. Is 27,969 at most 30,331? Yes. Both answers yes means inside, including a closing price that sits on the top line or the bottom line. Is 33,086 at most 30,331? No. One no means outside. You can do that with any later closing price. The two dates are the ones this lesson asks you to learn.

Write the card in the order you will say it, so the check has an ending. Line one: the date. Line two: inside or not, with the two comparisons. Line three: which side of 27,630, and only if the closing price is inside. Line four: in force, or fired. On 7 October, line three is allowed, and the answer is above, by 339. Line four is in force. The stop has not fired. On 23 October, you skip line three, because the closing price is outside, and line four is in force, with a warning, because this is the first closing price outside. On 24 October the next closing price, 33,902, is still outside, and line four is fired. When line four is written, that week's check is done. If the rule is still in force, you open the notebook again the next week and ask the same three questions of each new daily closing price since the last check. If the stop has fired, you do not. You follow the sentence you already wrote.

If you want the fourth row, label it before you write in it. Write the words "review record, not a target" at the top of that row. Then you may note a finished pair, coin still held, fees, and a yes or no for the rules. If you cannot say which of those four notes you are writing, leave the row blank. A blank review row is allowed. A blank answer to "inside or not" is not.

Check yourself. What are the three questions? The answer is: inside or not, which side of the middle, and whether the rule is still in force or the stop has fired. On 7 October 2023, is 27,969 inside 24,930 to 30,331? The answer is yes. On 23 October 2023, does which side of the middle still matter? The answer is no. The closing price has left the range. What is the fourth row? The answer is a review record, not a target.

On paper, or on a demo that uses pretend money, write the two dates and the three answers. Write Example beside both closing prices. Do not send an order. A finished check is not a new plan.

The middle is a side marker, not a third line of the range. The range is still 24,930 and 30,331. 27,630 only answers "which side" when the closing price is inside. On 7 October, 27,969 is 339 above it, so the side is above. On 23 October you do not use 27,630 at all. Using the middle on an outside day makes the page look as if the closing price were still in the range. It is not. Skip that question and go to the rule.

Recap. The second distance is outside it. You do not add them. You do not average them. Each one answers its own date. When both answers are written, monitoring for this lesson is finished.
