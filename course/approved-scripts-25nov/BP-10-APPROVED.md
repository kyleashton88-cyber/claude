Education only. Not financial advice. This is general information and doesn't consider your objectives, financial situation or needs. Crypto is high risk; you could lose all the money you put in. Past performance is not a reliable indicator of future results. The prices are public historical daily prices, marked Example. The range uses the high and the low of that window. When we say inside or outside, we use the daily closing price. No order is sent from this lesson.

Scaling on this page means a second written page, not a bigger feeling. Page one is the summer of 2023. Page two is a later window, measured on its own. The checks stay the same. The one change is the range. Every figure is marked Example. This is education only. No order is sent.

By the end you will open a second page only after the first page has a stop rule. You will change the range and nothing else. You will keep each page in its own bucket, meaning its own box with its own range and its own stop.

A daily closing price is the last price of the day. This lesson uses the end of each UTC day, the world's standard clock, which is 11am Sydney time in daylight saving, 10am otherwise. A range is the top line and the bottom line. A stop is the rule for a daily closing price outside that range, confirmed by the next daily closing price still outside. Spot means coins you hold. You buy them with money you already have. A multiple, such as five times, means borrowed size. That can be a financial product, and it is higher risk. This course does not teach it and does not recommend it. Scaling here is a second written spot page, not a multiple.

Page one is the summer window, 19 July 2023 to 16 October 2023. The bottom is 24,930. The top is 30,331. Thirty thousand three hundred and thirty-one minus twenty-four thousand nine hundred and thirty is five thousand four hundred and one. Six gaps of about 900 fit that width. The stop is already written. The closing price left the range on 23 October 2023, at 33,086, and the stop was confirmed on 24 October 2023, when the next closing price was still outside. Page two does not open until that stop exists. A page that cannot finish is not a base for a second page.

Page two is 27 March 2024 to 24 June 2024. On the card its lines are 56,555 and 72,715. Seventy-two thousand seven hundred and fifteen minus fifty-six thousand five hundred and fifty-five is sixteen thousand one hundred and sixty. That width is wider than the summer width of 5,401. If you want six gaps again, you divide the new width by six. Sixteen thousand one hundred and sixty divided by six is about 2,693. That figure is an Example of a recount. It is not an order, and it is not a reason to use six gaps. You might choose a different count. The point is that you measure this window on its own.

Look at the window before you call it a range. The later window is not a range just because you want a second page. On this Example, looking back after 24 June 2024, the closing prices in those dates stayed inside 56,555 to 72,715. Those two edges are the high and the low of the window, not the highest and lowest closing prices. You would not have known that on the first day of the window. This is hindsight, marked Example, and it is not a forecast. That is why the page can carry the word range, with the dates written next to it.
