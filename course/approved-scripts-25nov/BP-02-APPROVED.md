## GridBot Blueprint

GridBot Blueprint, lesson 02, What you are going to need.

## Disclaimer

Education only. Not financial advice. This is general information and doesn't consider your objectives, financial situation or needs. Crypto is high risk; you could lose all the money you put in. Past performance is not a reliable indicator of future results. The prices are public historical daily prices, marked Example. No order is sent from this lesson.

## Hook

Crypto is a digital coin, such as bitcoin. A range is the top and the bottom, a gap is the space between two lines, and the step is how wide that gap is. A stop is the rule for what you will do if the price leaves the range. Like a packing list before a trip, your page comes before any screen.

## Objectives

By the end you will fill in a seven-item checklist on paper.

You will keep every secret off that page.

You will mark the page Example so nobody can read it as an order.

## Seven items

The seven items are: a notebook, three words, range, levels and stop, the pair, paper or a demo account, no secrets, a written stop, and the word Example on every figure. Each item has a reason. The notebook is the plan you can check later. The three words stop you drawing a grid with no edges. The pair names what you buy and what you pay with. Paper or a demo is learner plates, or training wheels. No secrets keeps the keys off the page. The written stop is the action. Example marks a teaching number. For this course the pair is bitcoin priced in US dollars. A demo account uses pretend money. A daily closing price is the last price of the day. You are naming that public series, not choosing an app.

## The three words

Three words, before any number. Range is the top line and the bottom line. An order is an instruction to buy or sell that can wait for a price. Levels are the price lines where orders wait, like the rungs of a ladder, and a gap is the space between two of them. A stop is the rule for what you will do if a daily closing price finishes outside the range and the next daily closing price is still outside. The figures from lesson 1 are on the card, marked Example. If you can only say one of the three words, the page is missing a part.

## No secrets

A wallet is where your crypto is held. A seed phrase is the list of words that unlocks that wallet. A private key is a secret that can move your crypto. An API secret is a password that lets a program place orders on an account, and sometimes move money out of it. You never type a seed phrase, a private key, or an API secret onto this page. The chart has no balance on it. A public closing price is not your account. If a form asks for a secret, that form is not this lesson.

## Copy the path

You copy the range onto the page, not every day's prices. Copy the bottom line and the top line, with six gaps of about 900. Every closing price in that window stayed inside the range. Mark each figure Example.

## Worked check

Worked check: pair, range, gaps, stop, and Example. If the stop line is only a tick with no sentence, the page fails the check. The rule has to say: if a daily closing price finishes outside the range and the next daily closing price is still outside, the example is over.

## The page

A page is the notebook you can hold. An exchange is the app or website where people buy and sell crypto. The screen is the exchange. Lesson 1 ended by asking you to bring the page. This lesson is that page. Open the notebook before any screen.

Write today's date on the first line. Under it, write the word Example. Example means a real past price used for practice. It is not an order and not a forecast.

## The pair

Write the pair first. The base is what you buy. The quote is what you pay with. For this course the prices are the July to October example window, the public bitcoin closing prices from 19 July to 16 October 2023, so you buy the base, bitcoin, with the quote, US dollars, and that row is labelled BTC/USD. Every other row depends on the pair. We price in US dollars. Your exchange may charge fees in Australian dollars, so keep each in its own column. If a fee sum is in Australian dollars, that sum goes on its own line, labelled BTC/AUD. Do not put a US dollar price and an Australian dollar fee on the same row.

## The range

Range is the top line and the bottom line. In the July to October example window those lines are 24,930 and 30,331. Both figures are Example. Levels are the lines between them. Six gaps of about 900 dollars sit between the top and the bottom. Write the top and the bottom only after you have said what a range is. Then write one gap, and how you got it: the top minus the bottom, divided by the number of gaps.

## The stop

The stop sentence has to say what you will do. A bot is a program that places and cancels the waiting orders for you. Two daily closing prices outside the range, through the top or the bottom, and the second closing price still outside. Then you stop the bot, which cancels the waiting orders, and you follow the rule you wrote. Write that stop in one sentence.

## Account in Australia

A deposit is crypto or Australian dollars you send into an account at an exchange. An exchange in Australia asks who you are before you can deposit. That check is required by Australian anti-money laundering law, and the exchange runs it, not this course. You only upload identification inside the exchange's own app. This course does not name an exchange and does not show a balance. Any exchange offering crypto services in Australia must register with AUSTRAC, Australia's financial crime regulator, as a virtual asset service provider. AUSTRAC keeps a public register you can search. Check that register before you sign up. The register says the business is listed. It does not say your money is protected. Registration is not a safety guarantee. ASIC is Australia's financial services regulator. From April 2027, platforms that hold crypto for you need an ASIC licence, with a transition period to October 2027 for platforms already operating. Check ASIC for the current position. This lesson names only government bodies, never an exchange.

## Two-factor

A second code, as well as your password, is two-factor authentication. Turn that on before any real dollars. Then look for a paper or demo mode. Paper uses pretend money. That practice is lesson 7.

## Fees

A fill is when your waiting order actually trades. A maker fee is the charge when your order waits and someone else trades with it. A taker fee is the charge when your order fills straight away. A grid uses waiting orders, so read the maker fee first. Some bots also pay a taker fee when they first start. In this example both are 0.1 percent. On an example buy of 100 Australian dollars, 0.1 percent is 10 cents. The sell costs about 10 cents as well. The round trip is about 20 cents, which is 0.2 percent. This fee row is Australian dollars. It is not the July to October example window. If the gap is smaller than that 0.2 percent, each completed buy and sell costs more in fees than the gap is worth. To compare them, turn the gap into a percent by dividing it by the price. In the lesson 1 example, 900 divided by 27,630 is about 3.3 percent, well above 0.2 percent. Lesson 5 is where you test a gap against this fee. You do not pick a gap in this lesson.

## Stablecoin

A stablecoin is a coin built to stay worth about one US dollar. It can still lose that value. If your pair is priced in a stablecoin, every fill trades one coin for another, so every fill is a swap.

## Journal

A trading journal is a dated record of every bot: the plan, the changes, the fills, the fees, a review note of what happened, and whether you followed the rules. That note is not money you made. A stablecoin fill goes in the journal. The tax office treats a swap like a sale, so every swap goes in the journal. The journal is also the page you will open again in lesson 8 and lesson 10. For every transaction, including a buy, a sell, a swap, or a transfer, write the date and time, the amount, the value in Australian dollars at the time, the fee, which exchange or wallet, what it was for, and who the other side was. The columns are the date, the pair, the range, the gaps, the money in the bot, the stop rule, the fills, the fees, the Australian dollar value of each fill, a review note, and a yes or no for the rules. The Australian dollar value column is the one exception. It is for your tax records. Download your exchange's transaction history at least every three months. Keep those records for at least five years. This is general information, not tax advice. Leave two columns blank for now. One is for the fees. One is for whether you followed the rule. You fill those later. You do not fill them with a hope.

## Scams

Nobody from this course will ask for a seed phrase, a private key, or an API secret. If a message rushes you to send crypto, or asks for one of those, treat it as a scam. It is not this course.

## Moneysmart

ASIC's Moneysmart website is the public Australian guide for scam warnings. Check it yourself.

## Say the page

Now cover the chart and say your page out loud: the pair, the range, the gaps and the stop rule. Name the pair, the range, the gaps, and the stop rule, then stop. If a word on it has not been defined, stop and define it before you go on. A secret is a seed phrase, a private key, or an API secret. None of those go on this page. You write Example on your own notebook so a practice sketch is never later mistaken for a live plan.
