# Market strategy frameworks

A framework is a way to organise a question before any order exists. This page stays at that level. It does not give setups to copy, it does not name a level to trade, and it does not tell you to enter or exit. Structure is on the [market structure page](market-structure.md). Size and expectancy are on the [risk page](risk-academy.md).

## Regime first

Name the regime on a timeframe you write down: uptrend, downtrend, range, or a high-volatility break. Tools that fit one regime often fit another badly. A neutral grid is a range framework. A trend map is a sequence of swings. If you cannot mark the swings, you do not yet have a regime, and a framework will not invent one.

Switching the label after the fact, so the story still fits, is not a framework. It is a rewrite.

## Thesis and invalidation

Every framework Atlas will discuss has the same four slots:

1. The observation that would have to be true.
2. The print that means it is no longer true. That is the invalidation.
3. The cash at risk if that print arrives, from the position-size formula.
4. A review date or a time stop, so a stale idea does not sit forever.

A framework without an invalidation is a hope. A framework without a size is not yet a plan.

## Trend continuation, as a question

The question is: is the swing sequence still intact, and is the current push a correction inside it rather than a break against it? Higher highs and higher lows, or lower highs and lower lows, are the map. A change of character is the event that retires the map.

This framework does not say "enter on the pullback". It says: if you cannot point at the swing that must hold, you are not using the framework. You are watching candles.

## Range and mean reversion, as a question

The question is: has price been oscillating between a ceiling and a floor you can mark, and is the planned activity inside that band? Mean reversion is a description of some markets some of the time. It is not a rule that every extension must snap back.

A grid's range thesis belongs here. If price lives outside the band, the framework says to review the thesis, not to silently widen the band until the picture is comfortable again.

## Break and retest, as a description

A breakout is a move beyond a level or a range that had been containing price. A retest is a later return to that level. Some breaks continue, some fail, and some return and then fail. The framework's job is to make you write, in advance, which of those events you are even talking about, and which print would retire the idea.

Measured distances from old textbooks are descriptions of samples. They are not a destination for the current market.

## Carry, funding, and basis, as a cost

Funding, basis, and borrow rates are cash flows attached to an exposure. A framework that uses them starts by writing the cash flow down: who pays, how often, and what else can go wrong if the price leg moves. Collecting a funding credit while carrying liquidation risk is not a completed idea. It is one number beside a larger risk.

Delta-neutral means a small move in the underlying changes the book very little. Fees, funding flips, and a hedge that gets cut are still in the book. The [futures page](futures.md) is the contract language. This section only says: measure the carry, and do not let the carry stand in for the invalidation.

## Event and calendar risk

Some frameworks begin with a known clock: an expiry, an unlock, a data release. The framework question is what you will do if the event passes and the path is not the one you sketched, including doing nothing. A calendar is not a forecast. It is a time at which you agreed to look again.

## The review loop

After the fact, score the process separately from the result. Did the regime label match the swings you can still mark? Did the invalidation exist before the order? Was the size the size in the plan? A win that broke the process and a loss that followed it are different lessons. The place to store that score is a [journal](journal.md). One good or bad result does not validate a framework.
