# Journal and review

A journal is a record of what you did, why, and what the result was. Without it, expectancy is a feeling. This page is general information about what to write down. It is not a promise that keeping a journal improves results, and it is not personal advice. The formulas are on the [risk page](risk-academy.md). The habits that distort a review are on the [psychology page](psychology.md).

## Minimum fields

For each decision, a useful record has:

- The date, the market, and the timeframe of the map.
- The regime you named, in one line, and the swing that was the invalidation.
- Planned size, planned cash at risk, and the order type.
- Whether you followed the plan. A yes or no is enough. A paragraph of excuse is optional.
- The closed result in money and in R.
- MAE and MFE, defined below.
- One sentence on what you would repeat, and one sentence on what you would not.

Screenshots help only if the swings are still marked. A picture of a candle with no invalidation is a souvenir.

## MAE and MFE

MAE, maximum adverse excursion, is how far the position moved against you before it closed, usually in R. MFE, maximum favourable excursion, is how far it moved in your favour before it closed. The closed result is what you kept. The gap between MFE and the result is what the management rule gave back. The gap between MAE and the stop is whether the stop sat inside the noise or far beyond where the trade went.

Hypothetical example. A plan risked 100 quote units. The worst open loss before the close was 80 quote units, so MAE is 0.8 R. The best open gain was 150 quote units, so MFE is 1.5 R. The closed result was a gain of 40 quote units, which is 0.4 R. Those four numbers describe this invented trade and no live account. Illustrative example on hypothetical data. Not a recommendation to trade this asset or these levels.

## What a review is for

A review sorts the record. Separate decisions where the process was followed from decisions where it was not. Compute the average only on a sample you are willing to name, and write the sample size beside it. A positive average on a handful of rows is a description of those rows. It is not a forecast.

Look for repeated process breaks: size different from the plan, invalidation added after entry, timeframe changed after a loss. Those are the edits that change the next decision. A single dramatic win is a poor teacher, because it rewards the memory rather than the rule.

## Backtests are not the journal

A backtest applies a written rule to past prices. A journal records what a person did. Mixing them hides the difference between the rule and the behaviour. If you tune a rule on the same rows you then quote, you are describing overfitting, which the [glossary](glossary.md#overfitting) defines. A forward stretch, or a paper record made after the rule was frozen, is a different object from the sample you designed on.

## A cadence

A common study habit is to reread the last entries on a set day, with the process column first and the profit column second. The day is yours to choose. The order of the columns is the part that matters, because profit-first reading is how a broken process survives the review.
