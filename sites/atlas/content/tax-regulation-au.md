# Tax and regulation in Australia

This page is general information about where Australian official guidance lives. It is not tax advice, not legal advice, and not a ruling on any reader. It does not calculate a gain or a loss, and it does not tell you how to structure a transaction. Consider your own circumstances. A registered tax agent or a lawyer can apply the law to you. Atlas cannot.

Market Pros Pty Ltd does not claim to hold an Australian financial services licence or to be registered as a virtual asset service provider. The education library does not exchange assets or hold customer funds. The [disclaimer](disclaimer.md) is the short form of that limit.

## The ATO, on tax

The Australian Taxation Office publishes how it treats crypto asset investments, including what it describes as capital gains tax events and what records it expects. Start there, and use the pages it links onward, rather than a summary from a third party:

[Crypto asset investments](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/crypto-asset-investments)

The ATO's own material describes many disposals of crypto held as an investment as CGT events. Its list of examples includes a sale, a gift, a swap for another crypto asset, a conversion to fiat, and spending the asset. That is the ATO's published framing. This page does not decide whether any of it applies to you, whether an asset is a personal-use asset, or whether a business is being carried on. Those are questions for the ATO guidance and for advice about your facts.

Record keeping is also an ATO topic, including how long records are kept and that values are dealt with in Australian dollars under the method the ATO describes. Follow their records page from the link above. Do not treat a wallet screenshot as a complete record unless it actually holds the fields they ask for.

## ASIC, on financial products and consumer risk

Whether a digital asset or a related service is a financial product depends on the facts and on the Corporations Act. ASIC's Information Sheet 225 is written mainly for businesses, and it points consumers to Moneysmart:

[Digital assets: financial products and services (INFO 225)](https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/digital-transformation/digital-assets-financial-products-and-services)

[Crypto assets on Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/complex-investment-products/crypto-assets)

Moneysmart is ASIC's consumer site. It describes crypto as high risk and volatile, and it explains that protections under the financial-services laws apply only to the extent those laws cover the product or the service. A licence, where one exists, is not an endorsement and is not a statement that a loss cannot occur. Atlas does not check a reader's counterparty and does not tell you that any firm is licensed.

## AUSTRAC, on businesses that deal in virtual assets

AUSTRAC registers businesses that provide certain virtual asset services in Australia. The public name in AUSTRAC's 2026 material is virtual asset service provider. Registration is a legal status for those businesses. It is not a tax category, not a safety rating, and not a recommendation. The overview and the public register are linked from the [exchanges page](exchanges.md#registration-in-australia-as-general-information):

[VASP overview](https://www.austrac.gov.au/industry-and-business/your-industry/virtual-asset-service-providers/virtual-asset-service-providers-overview)

[VASP register](https://online.apps.austrac.gov.au/vaspr)

## What to do with a conflict

If a lesson, a calculator, or a social post disagrees with the ATO, ASIC, or AUSTRAC, the official page wins. Lessons go stale. If your situation is not the simple case the public page describes, that is the moment to stop using this library as the source and to ask someone who is allowed to advise you.
