# Macro and ETF flows

Macro, in this library, means the cross-market backdrop: policy rates, inflation prints, growth data, and the US dollar. ETF flow means the creation and redemption of shares in a listed fund that holds, or references, a crypto asset. This page is general information. It does not name a fund, an issuer, or a venue, and it does not turn a headline into an instruction.

## What the backdrop is for

Rates and the dollar change how expensive it is to fund risk, and how a basket of other currencies sits against the dollar. A consumer price index is a statistical agency's measurement, with a method, and markets often react to the print versus what was expected. None of that is a crypto entry rule. It is context you can write beside a structure map. If the map and the macro story disagree, the disagreement belongs in the notes. It does not get averaged into a signal.

**Risk-on** is a label for sessions when assets treated as risky are bid together. **Risk-off** is the mirror, toward cash and government bonds. Correlations that looked loose in calm periods often tighten on those days. A label applied after the move is a description. It is not a timing tool. The glossary entries are [CPI](glossary.md#cpi), [DXY](glossary.md#dxy), [risk-on](glossary.md#risk-on), and [risk-off](glossary.md#risk-off).

## What an ETF is doing

An exchange-traded fund is a fund whose shares trade on a securities market. Some funds hold bitcoin or ether through a custodian. The share is not the coin. The custodian is a counterparty, and the fund's rules are in its own documents.

**Creation** is the process that brings new shares into existence. **Redemption** takes shares out. Net flow over a window is creations minus redemptions, sometimes restated as an amount of the underlying coin. Whether that process causes the fund to buy or sell the coin depends on the product. A headline that says "flow" without the issuer's method is not yet a spot-market fact.

Authorised participants, market makers, and the fund itself can sit between the share trade and any coin trade. Inventory can absorb a creation for a while. A flow figure can also be revised. Read the primary filing or the issuer's methodology note before you treat the number as coins that changed hands that day.

## How not to use a flow print

A large creation is not a forecast of the next price path. A large redemption is not one either. Both are quantities, on a definition, for a window. Put them next to liquidity and to the swing map if you want context. Do not let them replace the invalidation on a plan.

Do not add flow numbers from different publishers until you know they count the same products and the same clock. Double counting is ordinary when one firm reports a share trade and another reports an estimated coin equivalent.

## A short study habit

When a macro print or a flow headline is doing work in your reasoning, write three lines:

1. The source, and the window.
2. What the publisher says the number includes.
3. What would still be true on your chart if the number were revised or ignored.

If you cannot fill line 2, the headline is not yet evidence. The [market structure page](market-structure.md) is the chart. This page is only the backdrop.
