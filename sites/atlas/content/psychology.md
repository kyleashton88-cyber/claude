# Trading psychology

This page is general information about habits that show up when people make decisions under uncertainty. It is not therapy, not a diagnosis, and not a method that removes emotion. The practical counter, where there is one, is a written rule you can check later. The record is the [journal](journal.md). The size rule is the [risk page](risk-academy.md).

## Process and outcome

A sound process can lose on a single decision. A broken process can win. If you only remember the profit, you teach yourself the wrong lesson. Process, here, means the steps you said you would follow: the map, the invalidation, the size, the order type, and the review. You can mark each one as followed or not. The market's next print is not one of those marks.

## FOMO

FOMO, fear of missing out, is the pull to act because a move has already happened and other people seem to be in it. The plan's invalidation gets skipped because the feeling is about being late, not about the swing. Naming it in the journal is more useful than dressing the chase up as a setup. The glossary line is [FOMO](glossary.md#fomo).

## Revenge trading

Revenge trading is a new decision taken to get a loss back, rather than because the plan allows it. Size goes up, or the stop disappears, because the last result hurt. The market does not owe the loss back. A pause you wrote down in advance, before the next order, is a process control. It is not a judgement of character. See [revenge trading](glossary.md#revenge-trading).

## Confirmation bias

Confirmation bias is noticing the chart, the comment, and the indicator that agree with the idea you already want, and skipping the swing that contradicts it. A useful journal column is the case against the decision, written in one sentence before you act. Knowing the name of the bias does not switch it off. The column is the switch you can actually see later.

## Loss aversion

Loss aversion is feeling a loss more strongly than a gain of the same size. It shows up as moving a stop wider to avoid locking the loss, and as cutting a winner because the open gain feels fragile. Stating the result in R, from the [risk page](risk-academy.md#r-multiples), makes the asymmetry visible. Visibility is not the same as a rule that blocks the click. The rule has to exist before the click.

## A run of results

A string of gains can feel like evidence that the next decision should be larger. A string of losses can feel like evidence that the next decision must win the money back. Both feelings skip the sample-size question. A handful of results swings hard if you add or remove one of them. The [expectancy](risk-academy.md#expectancy) section is where that arithmetic lives. This page only says: do not let the feeling set the size.

## What this page will not promise

It will not promise calmer decisions, a higher share of successful outcomes, or a change in results from reading it. Reading a definition is not practice. Practice, if you want it, is marking the process in the journal on decisions you actually made, including the ones you would rather not reread.
