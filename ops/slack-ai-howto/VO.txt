In the next three minutes, you'll ask our AI in Slack the right way — click by click.

Open Slack on your computer. Look at the left sidebar. Under Channels, you'll see the rooms we use every day.

For company-wide asks that several desks might see, open all-audience-asset-co. For a quick internal note, use staff-chat instead. Pick the channel that matches who needs to see the work.

Click into the message box at the bottom. Type the at symbol. Choose the bot from the list — for routing work across desks, that is usually Orchestrator Bot. You can also mention ChatGPT or Creative Studio when the job is clearly theirs.

Write one clear ask. Say what you need, who it is for, and the deadline. Attach a file or paste a link if it helps. Keep wording education-safe. Do not put client profit claims or unverified money numbers in the ask.

When the draft looks right, press Send. Your ask appears in the channel so Stewart and the team can see it.

Now click Reply in thread on that same message. Put every follow-up, file, and clarification inside the thread. Do not start a new top-level message for the same job — that splits context and slows everyone down.

When the bot answers, read the reply in the thread. If something is missing or wrong, reply in that same thread with the correction. Stay in one thread until the job is done or handed off.

Here is a simple pattern you can copy. For who it is for, name Stewart or the desk. For by when, give a day and time. For what, state the outcome in one sentence. That format keeps Orchestrator and Creative Studio aligned.

If the bot asks a clarifying question, answer in the thread. If the work needs Stewart's yes before publish or spend, say that clearly in the ask so nobody ships early.

Quick recap. Open the right channel. At-mention the bot. Write one clear ask with a deadline. Keep follow-ups in the thread. Read the reply there. That is how we use AI with Slack at Market Pros.
